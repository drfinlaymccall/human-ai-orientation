# Optional: speak a draft on your own computer

You can turn speech into editable text using a downloaded app and speech model. This is an optional setup to try if you prefer speaking. Typing, your device's usual dictation and fictional examples remain available; installing anything is not required to finish the course.

These instructions were checked against the developers' sources on **24 October 2026**. We have not tried them on a physical Mac or Windows PC yet. Accuracy, speed and available memory need checking on your own computer.

**On an Apple Silicon Mac:** [FluidVoice by Altic](https://github.com/altic-dev/FluidVoice) supports macOS 15 Sequoia or later. Its **Parakeet TDT v3** speech model supports English and 24 other languages. The model download is about 500 MB; allow about 1 GB of disk space for the speech model, plus room for the app and installation. The app's source lists 4 GB as the recommended minimum RAM for Parakeet, but that is not a tested memory guarantee. Intel Macs use a different speech model, Whisper, rather than this Parakeet route. [Check the current requirements](https://github.com/altic-dev/FluidVoice#requirements).

**On Windows:** [Handy by CJ Pais](https://handy.computer/) is a separate option with [official x64 and ARM64 installers](https://github.com/cjpais/Handy/releases/latest). Its Parakeet V3 model runs on the computer's processor and downloads about 478 MB. We have not verified a minimum Windows version or RAM requirement: check whether the current release works on your computer. FluidVoice's Windows beta is a separate, changing product and is not covered by this guide.

Both core apps are free. Speech recognition can work on your computer after the app and chosen model have downloaded. They still use the internet for downloads/updates; optional cloud text cleanup sends content elsewhere. FluidVoice also discloses weekly activity analytics, even when detailed analytics are switched off. Local audio/history may be kept: review the app's settings. Local transcription does not make a note stored in a cloud-synced notes app local-only.

1. **Install and prepare.** Download FluidVoice only from [Altic's official GitHub Releases](https://github.com/altic-dev/FluidVoice/releases/latest), or Handy from [its official Releases](https://github.com/cjpais/Handy/releases/latest). Follow onboarding, choose the microphone and download **Parakeet TDT v3** in FluidVoice or **Parakeet V3** in Handy. On Mac, grant the requested microphone and Accessibility permissions if you choose to proceed; Accessibility lets the app insert text into other apps. Set a shortcut you can remember. Leave optional AI enhancement/post-processing off. Keep **Spoken Send off** in FluidVoice or **Auto Submit off** in Handy.
2. **Speak into a draft.** Open a local text editor with cloud sync off, click in a blank document and use your chosen dictation shortcut. Use FluidVoice's ordinary Dictation mode. Say a sentence or two, then stop with the shortcut or the control shown by the app. Handy's push-to-talk mode stops when you release its shortcut. Start with something fictional: “The example appointment is on Tuesday at ten. Do not book anything.”
3. **Read and correct.** Check the day, time and “do not”, along with names and anything missing or added. Correct the text with the keyboard. Keep the words you mean. If nothing appears, check the microphone and selected model, then use typing or your usual dictation while you work it out.
4. **Choose where it goes.** Copy only the corrected text and paste it into the course box you intended. Saving portfolio text is a separate action. For feedback, open **Give feedback**, paste into **Add a note**, review it again and choose **Send feedback** only when you are ready. This sends the text to Finlay and authorised reviewers; it does not attach the app's audio unless you separately choose to upload an audio file.

To send your voice recording instead, use the feedback panel's existing **Record → Stop → listen → Send feedback** route, or upload an audio file. You can send typed text too. Text and audio upload remain available on the ordinary feedback page without JavaScript. Dictating text is different from sending a recording, and the course does not turn recordings into text automatically.

Official help: [FluidVoice setup and privacy](https://github.com/altic-dev/FluidVoice#quick-start), [Handy getting started](https://handy.computer/docs/), [Handy output/history settings](https://handy.computer/docs/advanced.html).
