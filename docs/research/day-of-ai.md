# Day of AI discovery and orientation integration ledger

Checked: 16 October 2026 (Australia/Sydney). Status: research and proposed integration; no learner-facing publication, resource import, account creation or email dispatch.

## Recommendation

Pilot an optional, untimed **one-question side quest** with useful feedback and a return to the learner's task. Start with choosing an appropriate AI task, selecting context, or verifying a claim. The strongest transferable feature of Day of AI is its question → explanation pattern. Its school-group delivery, timed scoring and leaderboard do not directly serve this adult, self-guided orientation.

Use newly authored orientation questions first, with acknowledgement of the format inspiration. Link to Day of AI for optional exploration. Adapt specific Day of AI questions or teaching assets only after inspecting their notices and recording the applicable licence. The site publishes CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 terms; attribution alone is insufficient, and adapted materials cannot simply inherit this project's CC BY 4.0 notice.

This supports the existing three activities and the proposed personal learning map. It does not add a fourth compulsory activity, change completion, or establish competence from trivia performance. The research recommendation is to pilot the homepage interaction before adding account-backed email personalisation.

## Reuse and attribution decisions

The [Day of AI terms](https://dayofai.org/terms-of-use) offer CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 for covered material and separately exclude trademark use. The site's noncommercial guidance specifically distinguishes internal training from selling courses based on its materials. A free page that supports a commercial offer should not be assumed cleared merely because access is free. The orientation's portfolio purpose and possible later paid programme make this worth resolving before actual imports.

| Use | Working decision |
| --- | --- |
| Link with our own concise description | Suitable immediate treatment; preserve source identity and avoid endorsement claims. |
| Independently author a different question using orientation tasks and the broad quiz-feedback pattern | Keep our original wording, examples and rationale. Acknowledge Day of AI as design inspiration; do not treat close paraphrases of its question bank as independent authorship. |
| Copy or adapt a covered question, explanation, worksheet or slide | Record item and creator, exact source, licence/version, changes and any excluded assets. Retain NC-SA conditions on the adaptation. |
| Mix adapted content with original CC BY materials | Mark the distinct third-party/adapted item visibly and in downloadable/source versions. Do not label it CC BY or assume every combination is a mere collection; review the actual form of reuse. |
| Use partner videos, images or linked tools | Check each item's terms. Linking is the initial recommendation; embedding, downloading, clipping and rehosting are separate decisions. |
| Reuse logos, character art or branding | No blanket permission established. Plain-text attribution avoids suggesting affiliation; use our own interface and visual assets. |
| Import quiz code, iframe or results into Moodle | No supported integration/export contract established. Content licensing does not by itself license the website's software or supply an API. |

Attribution template for a verified adaptation (replace brackets with the actual asset credits):

> Adapted from “[title]” by [credited creator], published by Day of AI ([source URL]), under [CC BY-NC-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/). Adaptation by Finlay McCall: [adult scenario, shortened wording, changed feedback and other actual changes]. This adapted activity is CC BY-NC-SA 4.0. No affiliation or endorsement is implied.

For a link: “Explore [resource title] from Day of AI”, with a direct source link. Preserve any additional named creators on the actual asset. “Powered by MIT” is not a course branding statement we should adopt.

Suggested record for each eventual question: stable ID; version; learning objective/can-do ID; source title/URL; actual creator; source checked date; source-versus-original status; licence and licence URL; asset exclusions; changes; correct option; explanation for each option; follow-up practice URL; accessibility alternative; publication/review status. Store learner responses separately from this public content bank.

## Proposed homepage and email experience

**Homepage:** an optional “Try a two-minute AI question” card below the main course action. Show one question, clear answer choices, an optional “I’m guessing” choice, and explanatory feedback. Let learners retry or skip. Offer one related course step and an optional Day of AI source link. Keep keyboard operation, text alternatives and a no-JavaScript route. Do not add speed penalties, public rankings, compulsory streaks or a new completion gate.

**Email:** send one question with ordinary answer links. A chosen answer opens the orientation's results page, displaying the selected answer, feedback, a suggested next activity and an easy way to change the answer. The full question must also be reachable through a plain “Open this question” link. This is a proposed flow, not currently implemented personalisation.

The design must explicitly distinguish selecting an answer link from recording a durable response. Use the first arrival to show a provisional result; require an explicit on-page confirmation before saving it. This avoids treating automatic email-link inspection or preview loading as a learner attempt. Implementation should verify this behaviour against the actual mail and web stack.

For personalised results, use the existing authenticated learner identity. If a narrowly scoped email token is added, it should be opaque and expiring, and should not expose an email address, learner name, portfolio or general account access in a URL. A forwarded link must not reveal private progress. Anonymous visitors get the same explanation without saved claims; an expired link returns safely to the public question or sign-in. Do not assume browser-local portfolio drafts are available across devices.

After confirmation, record question/version, chosen option, confidence/guess flag and learner-selected practice. “Answered correctly”, “viewed explanation”, “wants practice” and “can do independently” remain distinct. The quiz may recommend a map entry; it must not award practical competence or silently alter historical completion.

Email delivery depends on the existing [operations backlog](../operations/email-and-support.md): optional subscription, frequency/pause/unsubscribe controls and an appropriate return route. Sending a campaign or creating a new reminder schedule is not part of this research. Course email working for one owner-address test does not establish a bulk personalised quiz service.

## Three original pilot question sketches

These are newly authored orientation examples, not extracted Day of AI questions. Proposed stable IDs are local suggestions, not existing competency-bank IDs. Estimated duration: 1–3 minutes each.

| ID / placement | Question and choices | Answer, feedback and next step |
| --- | --- | --- |
| OQ-01 / useful task | You want help preparing for a meeting. Which first task is easiest to check? A: Ask AI to decide the team's priorities without background. B: Give a non-sensitive agenda and ask for three questions you can review. C: Ask it to invent missing meeting decisions. | B. A small task with relevant context gives you a result you can inspect. A leaves out the decision context; C creates unsupported facts. Next: choose a real small task and write a sign of success. |
| OQ-02 / useful context | You want a clearer version of a project update. What should you provide first? A: Every file in your work folder. B: Your account password so the AI can look around. C: A relevant non-sensitive draft, intended audience and desired change. | C. Select information that serves this request. A adds unrelated material and possible private data; B is unnecessary sensitive information. Next: review one paragraph of your context brief for relevance and remove anything you should not share. |
| OQ-03 / check and continue | An AI gives a precise deadline and a citation for your project. What is the best next step? A: Open the original source and check that it supports the date and applies to your situation. B: Accept it because the answer is specific. C: Ask for the same answer in a more confident tone. | A. Precision and confident wording are not verification. B and C leave the underlying claim unchecked. Next: verify one consequential claim in your own AI output and keep the source in your notes. |

Each answer should offer an explanation, including when correct. After feedback ask whether the learner guessed and whether they want to practise. Add a small real task to assess practical capability; these multiple-choice sketches alone cannot do that.

## Delivery proposal and evaluation

1. Review the three original sketches and their course links. Build one shared, versioned question representation for public and Moodle delivery when implementation is commissioned.
2. Pilot a single optional homepage question with anonymous feedback and explicit non-grading language. Preserve the main course entry and existing records.
3. Ask a small human pilot whether the question helped them choose or improve a task. Check keyboard/mobile/no-JavaScript use, comprehension of feedback and return-to-course behaviour. Synthetic checks can establish function only.
4. Select one Day of AI adaptation only if it adds value beyond our original questions; inspect the full asset and record rights and attribution before publication.
5. Add personal response storage and email return links once identity, preferences and the learning-map design are ready. Rehearse with synthetic capture mail before any real campaign.

Evaluate whether learners reach a useful next action, can explain the reasoning and transfer it to their task. Track confirmed responses separately from page views and email-link visits. Do not optimise for raw click-through or leaderboard scores as evidence of learning.

## Discovery completion notes

**World Food Day browser check:** completed the anonymous ages 11–13 route (five questions). Observed topics: prediction models; performance when growing conditions differ; underrepresented tomato varieties in training data; assessing a school food-demand model; and possible food-waste reduction. This topic inventory is a research summary, not an imported question bank. Each answer produced correctness feedback, the answer and a short explanation; the end showed a game score and a route back to more games. No per-question sources or share/embed/export controls were observed. Other age bands and evergreen question sets were not played through.

Use the written scoring instructions as the source of truth: up to 1,000 points per correct question, with speed affecting points. The browser tool loosely described a “base plus bonus”; no separate scoring algorithm was verified. Leaderboard participation requires an account; the public trivia hub itself does display leaderboard entries, so it should not be described as entirely hidden from anonymous visitors. No identifying information was entered and no score was submitted to a leaderboard.
