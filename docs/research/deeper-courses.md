# Comparable courses, supporting resources and reuse

Reviewed 13 October 2026. Public learner page: https://orientation.finlaymccall.com/deeper-courses. Native Moodle Page: module 4, after the context lesson in section 1. Shared original summary source: `content/deeper-courses.html`.

## Positioning and fit

This is a selective curriculum comparison, not an exhaustive market scan or evidence that no comparable course exists. The fit assessments below are design judgements grounded in published outlines; the courses were not completed during this review.

- **Closest conceptual neighbour:** Dakan, Feller and Anthropic’s AI Fluency: Framework & Foundations. Its delegation, description, discernment and diligence align strongly with purposeful human–AI collaboration and checking. The authors describe 12 lessons, 3–4 hours including about 70 minutes of video. It is broader than a Claude-interface tutorial. The hosted platform is Skilljar, not Skillshare.
- **Closest practical provider pathway:** Microsoft’s AI fluency on OpenLearn, with everyday tasks, responsible use and accessibility. Good beginner material, but demonstrations emphasise Copilot and may show older interfaces. It is proprietary despite its free OpenLearn hosting.
- **Short explanations to support a task:** Code.org’s How AI Works, especially the chatbot/LLM and computer-vision videos. The curriculum targets grades 6–12; individual explanations can still help adults. Do not import classroom framing wholesale into a general adult course.
- **Broader conceptual extension:** Elements of AI, University of Helsinki and MinnaLearn. Six chapters with reasoning/probability exercises; provider estimates 4–8 hours per part. This is a substantial optional next course, not a prerequisite for learning to give an instruction.
- **Social/ethical background:** Open University’s An introduction to artificial intelligence. Two-week structure, self-paced, focused on history, social implications and ethics rather than operating current chat apps.
- **Specific tool help:** OpenAI Academy’s ChatGPT 101 and current Academy resources. Linked recording is from 2025 and displays a sign-in/join gate. Do not imply its demonstrated UI or features are current/free in every account.
- **Later cohort pathway:** Khan Academy/Code.org/ETS/ISTE’s AI 101 for Teachers. Five free videos on educational practice. Appropriate if an educator cohort emerges; optional for the present general audience. The Khan Academy course page failed to render in the research fetch, so the readable official article with all five videos is the destination.
- **Digital foundations:** retain Be Connected as a prerequisite-support branch. Its already-verified computer basics course includes a video and transcript; not another required AI course.

A useful positioning for Finlay’s course is a guided practical route from ordinary computer controls to communicating instructions, carrying useful personal context and evaluating results with the learner’s chosen AI. Do not claim uniqueness on the basis of this shortlist. The design contribution is the combination, sequencing, scaffolding and evaluation of the learner experience. Provider or cohort pathways can branch later without changing the generic core.

OpenAI’s [OATS collaboration](https://openai.com/index/helping-older-adults-use-ai-in-everyday-life/) is an additional close example of hands-on everyday AI literacy. The inspected September 2026 page describes an in-person event in ten US communities, not a currently enrolable global self-access course. It is retained as a design comparator, not promoted as an available course in the learner table.

## Integration decisions

All resources are optional outbound links with original descriptions. No videos, slides, screenshots, transcripts or exercises from external providers are copied into this course. The initial AI-interface activity links to the guide; native Moodle has a Page after the first lesson, and preparation/notes pages link to it. Public navigation also provides access without a course account.

Use a small return loop: choose one question → study a relevant section → explain one idea in your own words → try it with your own AI → record what changed and retain the source link. A fictional reflection is explicitly labelled. External enrolments and completion do not change Moodle completion. Watching a video is not evidence of successful transfer.

Captions, transcripts and account behaviour vary. Course outlines and notices were inspected, not every media player, caption track or enrolment path. Do not promise universal no-login access or media accessibility. Offer reading options and preserve our own written instruction route.

For adaptations, record title, creator, source, licence/version, changes and exclusions for each item. Keep third-party credits separate from Finlay’s original content notice. A BY-NC-SA adaptation cannot be offered solely under CC BY; an unchanged linked resource is not relabelled by our licence. Review non-commercial conditions against the intended use, especially before paid cohort delivery. Code and LMS/vendor assets keep their software licences.

Finlay selected **CC BY 4.0** on 13 October 2026 for original course materials. `LICENSE-CONTENT.md` and the public/Moodle scope notice define the grant, exclusions and attribution. Third-party materials and software retain their own rights; learner/private data and screenshots are excluded.

## Primary sources and verification notes

- [Anthropic course](https://www.anthropic.com/ai-fluency): curriculum and free registration; redirects to Skilljar.
- [AI Fluency authors](https://aifluencyframework.org/): scope, timing and CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 link, including videos. The separate Anthropic PDF found in search was for Teaching AI Fluency, so it was not used as the sole licence proof for Foundations.
- [Code.org video-series page](https://code.org/en-US/curriculum/how-artificial-intelligence-works): free beginner videos and school-oriented lessons; links to [official YouTube channel](https://www.youtube.com/codeorg). A candidate direct Anthropic YouTube video did not yield usable metadata and was not added as a verified media link.
- [OpenLearn Microsoft course](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/digital-computing/ai-fluency) and [OU introduction](https://www.open.edu/openlearn/education-development/ai-matters): current learning outcomes and account features, plus asset-specific acknowledgements above.
- [Elements overview](https://course.elementsofai.com/), [authors/home](https://www.elementsofai.com/), [time estimate](https://www.elementsofai.com/faq/how-many-hours-will-the-course-take), [certificate FAQ](https://www.elementsofai.com/faq/i-have-passed-an-elements-of-ai-course-where-is-my-certificate-2): distinguish the free Introduction course from Building AI’s paid optional certificate. The learner table makes no blanket certificate promise.
- [OpenAI free hub announcement](https://openai.com/global-affairs/scaling-the-openai-academy/), [Academy](https://academy.openai.com/), [ChatGPT 101](https://academy.openai.com/public/videos/chatgpt-101-a-guide-to-your-super-assistant-2025-02-13): free resource offering, present navigation and recording sign-in prompt. The AI Foundations page exposed only navigation, so its unverified syllabus was not summarised.
- [Khan Academy’s official five-part series article](https://blog.khanacademy.org/khanmigo-ai-101-for-teachers-5-part-video-series/): format, free access and session topics.
- [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) and [CC BY-NC-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc-sa/4.0/): attribution, adaptation and licence conditions.

Review access, links, outlines, interface age and licence notices during weekly maintenance before any deeper integration. Keep documentation-based recommendations separate from eventual first-hand course and learner evaluations.
