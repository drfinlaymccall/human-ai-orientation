A task is a particular action inside an activity, such as selecting a relevant detail or reopening a saved brief.

### How activities, tasks and competencies connect

- **Activity/module:** the place the learner chooses, such as “Give AI the right background”. It provides explanation, examples and practice.
- **Task:** what the learner actually does there, such as explaining an inclusion/omission choice or correcting an invented date.
- **Competency:** what the learner learns to understand and do through those tasks. In the interface these appear as “What you'll practise” and “Check what I can do”.
- **Capability grouping:** an author's way of organising related competencies across activities, such as communicating with AI or evaluating results. Several activities can develop the same capability.

For example, “Give AI the right background” helps a learner select relevant information and communicate it. One task asks them to notice where the response conflicts with their instruction, clarify it and judge the revision. The associated knowledge check asks them to explain the mismatch and a suitable clarification strategy. “Check a result and plan your next step” revisits those skills in a fresh task. The same competency is referenced rather than duplicated.

### What opens when a learner selects a place

Example selected place: **Give AI the right background**.

**You'll do:** choose a useful detail, write a short brief, try it with your AI and work through anything it gets wrong about your intention.

**You'll keep:** a brief you can reuse in another conversation.

**What you'll practise:** choosing relevant information; explaining your intention; identifying and repairing a misunderstanding.

**Check what I can do:** “Can I explain why a detail helps?” and “Can I spot a mismatch, clarify it and check the revision?” Knowledge and practical judgements can differ. The full knowledge/capability statements remain available in plain language when the learner expands a self-check.

Primary action: **Open this activity**. A worked-example option supports exploration before trying. Any saved-progress or assessment control remains a proposed feature until implemented; a mockup must identify example controls as a preview.

## The model we are using

Recommended description: **a needs-led, task-based human–AI orientation, with can-do competencies, experiential practice and formative self-assessment**.

There are four distinct layers:

1. **Curriculum foundation:** Finlay's curriculum/course-design research and its orientation adaptation: needs, content/knowledge, evaluation and execution. These interact and recur; they are not four compulsory learner modules. The thesis's five-domain framework and the later four-pillar adaptation remain distinct. See the existing [scholarly rationale](../research/scholarly-design-rationale.md).
2. **Capability framework:** six areas describe what the learner becomes able to do. Each competency pairs a knowledge outcome with an observable capability, aligned to both dimensions of revised Bloom. A capability can be practised in several modules; a module can develop several capabilities.
3. **Learning experience:** choose a useful task, inspect an explanation/example, try it, reflect on the result, identify a useful principle and test an adjustment. Learners may return to any capability. Guidance and examples remain available before practice.
4. **Assessment:** compare a particular attempt with a small success criterion, record the help used and decide what to practise next. The purpose is learning and useful judgement, not certification or a single proficiency score.

The six areas are a course-specific proposal, not an existing external standard. Revised Bloom supplies the two-dimensional classification used to articulate and align each competency: knowledge content and the cognitive work done with it. It is not a required learner sequence. Experiential learning describes how practice, reflection and another attempt connect; experience alone does not establish capability.

These are orientation-sized performances. “Independently” means choosing and carrying out the action without another person directing each step. Using an AI, an accessibility aid or an ordinary reference does not automatically mean dependence; record what support was needed. Copying a worked answer alone does not demonstrate the intended skill.

## Paired knowledge outcomes and Bloom alignment

Each row below completes the competency with the same ID in the bank above. The **knowledge can-do** makes the relevant content and understanding inspectable; the bank's **capability can-do** asks the learner to use it in a task. Together with the evidence and criterion, these form one competency. Learners can explain something yet still need help performing it, or perform a familiar routine while being unable to explain when it is appropriate. Record that difference rather than awarding both from one tick.

Knowledge types: **A factual**, **B conceptual**, **C procedural**, **D metacognitive**. Cognitive categories: **1 Remember**, **2 Understand**, **3 Apply**, **4 Analyse**, **5 Evaluate**, **6 Create**. Subprocess numbers refer to revised Bloom. Multiple cells are appropriate where the task genuinely involves several processes. This author mapping is a proposal about the specified tasks, not a psychometrically tested classification.

The knowledge dimension includes knowing how and when to use a method. **Procedural knowledge is not automatically Apply**, and metacognitive knowledge is not automatically Evaluate: a learner can recall a procedure or explain a self-monitoring strategy. Conversely, an explanation is not proof that the learner can carry the procedure out. Assess the requested cognitive work on the specified knowledge.

### Worked revision: identify a misunderstanding, then repair it

Area summary: **I can communicate my intention, identify and explain misunderstandings, choose a clarification strategy and evaluate whether it worked.**

TALK-02 has three separately reviewable capability subchecks under the same stable ID:

1. **Diagnose:** I can interpret the response and identify the particular words, omissions or assumptions that conflict with my intended meaning.
2. **Repair:** I can select and use an appropriate strategy—paraphrase, example, question or explicit constraint—to address that mismatch.
3. **Evaluate:** I can judge the revised response against my intended meaning and explain what is resolved or remains unclear.

For the knowledge check, ask the learner to explain the relevant meanings and why their chosen strategy could address the mismatch. A plausible explanation of the breakdown is not proof of the model's internal cause or mental state.

Fictional example: a community invitation must leave date and venue undecided, but the AI supplies a date. The learner identifies the unsupported detail, explains its conflict with the instruction, asks for a placeholder and checks the revision for invented arrangements. Naming the mismatch is distinct from producing the repair. TALK-02's working scale should expose the three subchecks; completing one must not silently establish the others or create three compulsory new modules.

### Coverage across the spectrum

This communication example shows how the same area can elicit all six categories at orientation depth. It is an authoring check, not a compulsory six-step ladder for every competency.

| Cognitive category | Illustrative communication objective | Knowledge used and evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Remember | Recall the task's stated audience and boundaries. | Factual details; name them before reviewing the answer. |
| Understand | Paraphrase the AI's response and explain what it appears to mean. | Conceptual knowledge of intended/expressed meaning; paraphrase and explanation. |
| Apply | Use a demonstrated clarification procedure in a familiar exchange. | Procedural knowledge; perform the procedure. |
| Analyse | Distinguish the intended constraint from the conflicting part of the response. | Conceptual/procedural knowledge; mark the relevant mismatch and its relationship to the intention. |
| Evaluate | Judge whether the revision satisfies the original constraint. | Conceptual/procedural knowledge; judgement against an explicit criterion. |
| Create | Devise an alternative clarification or example for a breakdown the supplied routine does not resolve. | Conceptual/procedural knowledge; produce and explain a tailored solution rather than reproduce a supplied answer. |

Across the bank, Remember is explicit in knowledge checks; Understand includes interpreting, exemplifying, classifying, summarising and explaining; Apply includes executing/implementing; Analyse includes differentiating/organising; Evaluate includes checking/critiquing; Create includes generating/planning/producing. Breadth is checked across the course, not imposed equally on every row. Generating a small plan can count as Create without implying expertise, and writing text does not automatically count as Create. Ordinary comparison may be Understand (2.6); evaluating a comparison against a success criterion requires a different judgement. “Identify” and “clarify” have no fixed level apart from the actual task.

Bloom's cognitive complexity and the self-assessment support scale remain separate. A learner may need help recalling something and independently evaluate a familiar result. Do not convert Bloom categories into learner levels, map gates or a single competence score.

## Self-assessment and evidence

Keep the existing proposed response choices:

- **Not tried** — no judgement from an attempt yet.
- **Can do with help** — attempted with step-by-step support; note the support used.
- **Can do independently** — can choose and carry out this action in the stated situation without someone directing each step.

Allow **Not sure yet** as an explicit uncertainty response and **I want to practise this** as an independent choice available with any rating. A learner who skips assessment has an **unknown** rating; do not silently record “Not tried” or failure. Existing capability can be recorded at entry, with a dated baseline distinct from later updates. These are task-specific self-judgements, not universal mastery claims or externally calibrated levels.

For each claim, retain the competency ID/version, whether the judgement concerns its knowledge outcome or practical capability, rating, task/context, date, support used, evidence basis and chosen next practice. Retain component evidence for composite skills such as TALK-02, so a successful repair is not inferred from merely identifying the problem. A short learner-owned evidence note is enough initially; uploading private documents should not be necessary. Useful basis labels are **Self-report**, **Task evidence recorded**, and **Optional AI feedback**. These labels describe what the record contains, not verification or certification. Learner-reported performance and directly observed performance remain distinguishable. Keep earlier ratings when a learner updates them. V1.1 refines the draft definitions; a future implementation must retain the version attached to any claim rather than silently reinterpret earlier records.

Keep these separate on screen and in storage:

| Record | What it says | What it cannot establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Resource engagement | A specific observable event, such as opening a page | Reading, understanding or capability |
| Activity completion | The existing Moodle completion condition was met | Every related skill is independently demonstrable |
| Self-assessment | What the learner currently judges they can do | An externally verified proficiency level |
| Task evidence | A particular attempt/result and its stated basis | General competence in every setting |
| Course evaluation | Whether the experience helped or was clear | Learner capability |

For the optional starting-point check, a correct answer can suggest practice or a practical check. Let learners flag guesses, revise suggestions and visit any area. Knowledge questions cannot automatically award practical competence. Optional AI feedback uses an explicit task and criteria, waits for the learner's attempt and is reviewed by the learner. It does not silently update a rating or course completion.

> **Check what I can do** — expand to review the relevant knowledge and practical self-checks.

This is proposed interface copy, not a real learner record. On a visitor map, show “What you'll practise” without personal ratings. On the eventual signed-in map, the same activity destinations remain, with actual completion separately labelled; detailed skill judgements appear in self-checks or “My skills”. A portable skills view can retain reviewed self-assessments, next practice and public resource links without exposing private Moodle records.

Use real text and semantic links/buttons over or beside illustration. A bitmap cannot supply working links or saved progress by itself. Public links can go directly to the existing public activities; Moodle destinations must be resolved from the actual course configuration, not invented IDs. A responsive list provides the same destinations and outcomes on mobile, with keyboard access and without JavaScript. Status uses words and icons as well as colour. The full artwork can also be exported as an overview, with smaller area illustrations for module pages.

## Review and delivery boundary

This pass authors the bank, paired knowledge outcomes, two-dimensional Bloom alignment, activity mapping, assessment rules and visual briefs. V1.1 made misunderstanding diagnosis explicit and covered the revised-taxonomy spectrum. V1.2 responds to confusion between codes, capability groups, modules and tasks: it leads with the activity-based learner atlas and separates a readable course-design view from the technical references. An in-chat clickable prototype explores those two views; it is not deployed course navigation. No public page, Moodle activity, completion rule, database or learner record changes. Saved records/quiz still need their own implementation and storage design.

Documentation impact: this is author/design guidance, linked from the design brief and CONTROL.md; the discovery log records the request, draft, checks and limits. Current learner/operator guidance is unchanged because there is no live feature change. Keep the release-baseline work separate from this proposal.

Before rollout: finish the page/resource inventory, add missing evidence prompts, pilot whether learners understand the statements and can choose useful practice, confirm the storage/export/return design, and verify public/Moodle navigation, mobile layout, keyboard use and the no-JavaScript alternative. First atlas comparisons should use the same activity destinations and takeaways, with no invented personal ratings. Estimates already attached to existing activities are guides; do not invent separate skill times or infer a total from duplicated links.

Open review points: final wording and density of the 18 core statements; whether “Not sure yet” belongs alongside the three original ratings; final atlas copy/artwork and the saved-tracking design. The activity-led atlas direction is accepted and scheduled in the forward plan; remaining refinements do not reopen that choice or create a new launch date.
