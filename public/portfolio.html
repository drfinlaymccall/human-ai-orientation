My context

Activity 2 extension · Build and keep · About 20–30 minutes

Brief your new assistant.

Imagine an AI assistant has just joined you. It can help you draft, explain and organise things, but it has not met you yet. What would you tell it so you can work well together?

Start my first briefing

You are showing your assistant the ropes

Picture a first conversation: “Here is a little about me, how I like to work, and what I would like your help with.” Your assistant cannot reliably guess your preferences, priorities or what you mean by a good result. Give it concrete details and examples, as you would when briefing someone new.

You might talk about your interests, everyday responsibilities, a team or community you belong to, things you enjoy or find frustrating, and something you hope to achieve. Choose what would help this assistant work with you. A few useful details are a better start than your whole life story. Leave private or irrelevant information out; fictional answers are welcome too.

You are making a briefing you can keep, update and choose from for later conversations. It does not automatically give an AI lasting memory, access to your files or permission to act for you. You decide which parts to share and check what the AI actually uses.

  1. Start with the first three questions in Working with me. Type or speak a sentence or two to your assistant for each one. “I am not sure yet” is a useful answer.
  2. Read it back and copy Working with me. Paste it into a fresh AI conversation. Notice what helps and what you want to change. When signed in, choose Save to My context to keep it in Moodle.
  3. Add to it when there is a reason. The project and reference sections can wait until you have a specific task or useful material to keep.

An interview prompt to copy

Select and copy the text below. Sending it to your AI starts a conversation in that service; this course does not connect to it.

Let's role-play your first day as my AI assistant. Help me make a short Working with me briefing in my own words.
Ask one focused question at a time: what would help you understand my interests or everyday situation; how I would like you to communicate; and what you should check with me before doing or assuming. Offer a concrete example if I am unsure. Let me skip any question or use a fictional situation. Do not ask for sensitive details or information about other people. Do not infer traits, diagnoses or facts I have not supplied.
After those three answers, show a short draft using only what I said. Ask me to correct or remove anything. Separate facts from guesses, and mark unknowns. Ask whether I want to stop and save it or add a specific goal/project. Only ask about reference information or examples if we have something useful to include.
Show any further drafts separately as My goals and projects or Useful knowledge and examples. Remind me to save my own copy and share only relevant parts in future conversations. Do not claim lasting memory, file access or permission to act on my behalf.

Editing keeps a temporary draft in this browser tab. When signed in, choose Save to My context to keep your work in Moodle. Nothing is sent to an AI. Without an account, keep a private backup before closing the tab or changing devices.

Review: [what I corrected, date, what still needs checking]

A little help while you write

Finlay’s Tips

A sentence or two is enough to begin. Use a writing box or your own notes.

Say it first, then check it

Click in the box and use your device’s dictation to speak your answer instead of typing. Stop, read the words and correct anything it misheard. Keep only what you mean.

How to start voice typing

Windows 11: click in the box, then press Windows key + H. Select the microphone to stop. It needs a microphone and internet access. Windows setup guide (opens a new tab).

Mac: turn on Dictation in System Settings → Keyboard. Click in the box, then use the shortcut shown there or Edit → Start Dictation. Press Escape to stop. Mac setup guide (opens a new tab).

Phone or tablet: if your keyboard offers a microphone, use it to dictate into the selected box. You can also speak a draft in your usual AI app, review it there, then paste the parts you choose here. Typing, pasting and fictional examples work too.

These writing boxes do not record your microphone. Dictation uses your chosen device or app; check its settings if you are unsure what it sends or keeps.

Prompts you can reuse

Copy a starter into your own AI, then add only the context you choose. These buttons copy text; they do not contact an AI.

Help me make a clearer request 
Help me clarify this request. Ask me one question about an important missing detail, then help me rewrite it using my answer. Keep it simple and do not invent information. Wait for my rough request.
Interview me one question at a time 
Help me think through a short answer in my own words. Ask me which question I am answering, then ask one focused follow-up at a time. Give an example if I am stuck. Let me skip or use a fictional situation. Use only what I tell you, and show me a draft to correct before I keep it.
Check and improve a result 
Help me review a result against my goal. First ask for my goal, the result and what I have checked myself. Point out possible gaps or guesses and suggest one improvement. Do not claim you have verified facts you cannot check. Wait for my input.
More voice-input options

Finlay’s tip · Say it first, then tidy it up

Try speaking a first draft into these boxes. Dictation, also called voice typing, turns what you say into written words. Set it up on your computer so you can use it here and whenever you want to get an idea down.

  1. Choose one box and read its question. Start with “How to communicate with me” if you are unsure.
  2. Start dictation and say one or two sentences. For example: “Please use plain language. Give me one step at a time and wait for my answer.”
  3. Stop dictation, read the words and correct anything it misheard. Keep only what you mean and want to share.
Set up dictation on Mac or Windows

Mac: open the Apple menu → System Settings → Keyboard → Dictation, then turn it on. Click inside a text box and use the shortcut shown in those settings, or choose Edit → Start Dictation. Press Escape when you have finished. Apple’s dictation setup guide (opens a new tab).

Windows 11: click inside a text box, then press the Windows key + H. You need a working microphone and an internet connection. Follow the first-use prompts, wait for “Listening…” and speak. Select the microphone button to stop. Microsoft’s voice typing guide (opens a new tab).

Prefer to start in your AI app? If it offers dictation, speak your draft there, check the written words, then copy and paste the parts you want into the boxes here. Some free AI apps offer this: Claude’s mobile dictation includes its free plan (opens a new tab). Options vary by app, device and plan.

Practise with a short, ordinary example first. Get comfortable starting, stopping and correcting the text. You can always type instead.
