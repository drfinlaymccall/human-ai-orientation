Bring your own AI

Free AI tools are enough to get started.

Already use an AI you like? Start there. Otherwise, choose one of the options below. You only need one, and you can change later.

Start by giving a useful instruction: type it, say it, or add a file or picture to work with. Then check the answer and ask for a change. The free options below support that kind of work. You keep your context portfolio in your own notes.

Choose somewhere to start

Open a tool’s website and choose its free option if asked. Your AI account is separate from your course account. Availability can depend on your country, age and account type.

Choose one free AI app you can access. You do not need to choose a particular model inside it. These are ways to send information in: a photo upload is different from asking the AI to create a picture.

Sources and practical limits for each tool
  • ChatGPT: Free plan, voice and dictation distinction, supported uploads. Voice and uploads have separate allowances.
  • Claude: plans, mobile dictation, voice mode, documents and images. Free usage varies. File format matters; paste short notes if the file is rejected.
  • Gemini: mobile input shortcuts, Gemini Live, uploads, free-plan limits. Uploading requires sign-in; Live requires the mobile app.
  • Meta AI: official features and free-access FAQ. Meta lists text, voice, photos and documents, but does not give a complete free-plan/device matrix here. Verify the controls in your app; the table does not promise every feature in every Meta app.

What about Muse from Meta?

Muse is a separate personal agent with a free usage allowance. It can chat and create documents, as well as take actions. Meta’s launch announcement describes a US rollout; we have not confirmed broader availability.

If Muse is already available to you, try the text-only check below. For this lesson, ask it to draft in the chat. You do not need it to send anything, connect other apps or take actions for you.

Practise giving an instruction

Try the guided text, voice, file and picture activities, or use the quick text route below.

Paste this into your chosen tool:

Draft a friendly, short invitation to a fictional community gathering. The date and venue are undecided. Use [date] and [venue] as placeholders. Write the draft here in our chat; do not send or publish it.

Then ask: “Make it shorter and keep the placeholders.” If you can send both messages and read the replies, you have the basic interaction needed for the lesson. Check whether it kept the placeholders: a mistake is something to notice and improve.

You can paste the course context too. Web browsing, file uploads, saved memory and special project features are optional. The personal and project instructions you create can stay in your notes and be pasted into a chat.

What if I hit a limit or cannot access a tool?

Save your brief and return when the allowance resets, or use the lesson’s worked example. You can also paste your brief into another available tool. You do not need to buy a subscription to finish.

When you want to explore further

Finlay’s recommendation: Once you have had a play and found something useful, I strongly recommend trying a paid plan if it fits your budget. It can give you more room to experiment with advanced models and tools, and discover what today’s AI can do.

What you gain depends on the provider and plan: broader model access, additional features or higher limits. Compare the current ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini plans against something you actually want to try. Paying does not guarantee a correct answer, and it is optional for this course.
