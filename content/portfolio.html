Step 3 of 4 · About 20–30 minutes · Your three context documents

Save and reuse context.

Imagine an AI assistant has just joined you. It can help you draft, explain and organise things, but it has not met you yet. What would you tell it so you can work well together?

Why this helps: keeping reviewed notes lets you find, edit and share the relevant background in another conversation, instead of relying on the AI to remember it. These three documents are the main thing you take away from this course.

Start my first briefing

You are showing your assistant the ropes

Picture a first conversation: “Here is a little about me, how I like to work, and what I would like your help with.” Your assistant cannot reliably guess your preferences, priorities or what you mean by a good result. Give it concrete details and examples, as you would when briefing someone new.

You might talk about your interests, everyday responsibilities, a team or community you belong to, things you enjoy or find frustrating, and something you hope to achieve. Choose what would help this assistant work with you. A few useful details are a better start than your whole life story. Leave private or irrelevant information out; fictional answers are welcome too.

You are making a briefing you can keep, update and choose from for later conversations. It does not automatically give an AI lasting memory, access to your files or permission to act for you. You decide which parts to share and check what the AI actually uses.

  1. Start with the first three questions in Working with me. Type or speak a sentence or two to your assistant for each one. “I am not sure yet” is a useful answer.
  2. Read it back and copy Working with me. Paste it into a fresh AI conversation. Notice what helps and what you want to change. Paste it into your own notes as well, so you keep a copy.
  3. Add the other two documents. My goals and projects needs one task you would like help with. Useful knowledge and examples can stay short until you have material worth keeping.

Choose how to begin

Write it myself: answer the questions in the boxes below, typing or using your device’s dictation. Everything is optional.

Talk it through with my AI: copy the prompt below into your AI app. If the app has a microphone or voice button, answer out loud. Your AI asks the questions and writes up what you said. Read each draft and correct it. Then paste the parts you want to keep into the boxes below, or straight into your own notes.

Sending the prompt starts a conversation in that AI service. This course does not connect to it or see what you say.

Interview me so I can write three short context documents in my own words. I may answer by voice, so expect rough spoken answers. Tidy them, but do not add anything I did not say.
Ask one question at a time and wait for my answer. Offer an example if I am unsure. Let me skip any question or use a fictional situation. Do not ask for sensitive details or information about other people. Do not infer traits, diagnoses or facts I have not supplied.
Part 1, Working with me: what would help you understand my interests or everyday situation; how I like things explained or written; and what you should check with me before doing or assuming.
Part 2, My goals and projects: one task I would like help with; what a useful result would look like; any limits or undecided details; and one small next step.
Part 3, Useful knowledge and examples: a note, fact or example that would help with that task, where it came from and when I last checked it. Skip this part if I have nothing yet.
After each part, show a short draft using only what I said. Mark guesses and unknowns, and ask me to correct or remove anything. Ask whether I want to stop there or go on.
Finish by showing each document as plain text under its own heading: Working with me, My goals and projects, Useful knowledge and examples. Remind me to save my own copy and to share only the relevant parts in later conversations. Do not claim lasting memory, file access or permission to act on my behalf.

Editing keeps a temporary draft in this browser tab only. Before you close the tab, copy your documents into your own notes. When signed in, you can also choose Save to My context to keep your work in Moodle. Nothing is sent to an AI.

Keep it somewhere you can find again

Copy it into your own notes. Copy each document, paste it into the notes app or folder you already use and give it a name you will recognise. That copy is yours and works with any AI. Set up a place for your notes if you do not have one.

Other ways to keep a copy

A file: download a Markdown (.md) document, find it in Downloads and move it into your notes folder. Markdown is ordinary text with headings. Downloaded files do not update automatically.

A course account: when signed in, choose Save to My context, then find your documents in My context. Editing a box alone does not save it to Moodle.

A backup for this builder: the private JSON backup restores every box here later. It includes your practice record, so do not paste the whole backup into an AI. Restoring a backup changes this tab’s draft; when signed in, choose Save to My context to update Moodle.
