Step 4 of 4 · About 15–20 minutes

Check, improve and continue.

A portfolio is useful when you can find it again and use the right part. Try yours in a fresh conversation, check the result and keep one improvement. Then choose the next goal you want to complete with AI’s help.

Why this helps: reopening your work and checking a fresh result shows what is worth keeping, what needs correction and what you want to practise next.

Starting here?

Bring a task, a short context note and a result you want to check; you do not need to complete earlier activities first. If you have none to hand, use this fictional task: write a welcoming invitation under 100 words for a community gathering; the date and venue are undecided. The fictional invitation below gives you a result to inspect without an AI account.

Keep your observations in the practice record or your own notes. You can make a context document when you have an instruction worth keeping, or use a document you already have.

1. Start a fresh conversation

  1. Open the documents you saved from the portfolio builder. If you have a JSON backup, restore it there.
  2. Choose a small task and a success criterion. You can repeat the first task.
  3. Select only the context that helps. Copy that text into a new conversation in your AI tool, followed by your request.
  4. Ask the AI to say which supplied details it used and which important details are missing. Check that explanation yourself; it is not proof.

A new conversation may still have account-level memory or instructions. Check your tool’s settings and note anything you cannot rule out. A filename or link alone does not show that the AI read your content.

2. Inspect a specific result

Compare the answer with your original criterion and first attempt. Check at least one detail against your own information or a reliable source. Separate “sounds good” from “meets my criterion.”

Fictional example: checking an invitation

Criterion: under 100 words, welcoming, with the date and venue left undecided. A draft that says “Saturday at the library” fails the last part. Correct it: “The date and venue are not set. Replace both with placeholders. Do not invent arrangements.” Count the words and inspect the revised placeholders yourself.

If you cannot use an AI now, compare this example with the fictional first attempt. Record it as fictional practice, then return for your own test later.

3. Make one improvement and try again

Ask for a specific correction. Check the revised result. Then decide what to do with it: use it, revise it yourself, reject it, or get help from someone who knows the subject. Rejecting a result is a sound decision when it would not meet your criterion or you cannot check it. If the instruction helped, add it to “Working with me” or your project document using the portfolio builder. Keep it scoped: an instruction for invitations need not apply to every task.

4. Choose your next goal

Finish by choosing one thing you want to get done with AI’s help. Pick something that matters to you and that you could start within a week or two. A small real goal is more useful than a large vague one.

  1. Name the goal and why it matters to you. The reason helps you judge the result later.
  2. Decide your own part. What do you need to learn, choose or check yourself?
  3. List what the AI will need. Which of your three documents helps? What else must you tell it?
  4. Choose a first step you can check. Make it small enough to do in one sitting.
Fictional example: a next goal

Goal: send the book-swap invitation to my neighbours before the end of the month. It matters because last time only four people came. My part: confirm the date and venue with the organiser, and decide the final wording. The AI needs: my Working with me document, the confirmed date and venue, and the invitation I already checked. First step: ask for two versions under 100 words and check both against the confirmed details.

Write your answers in the last four boxes of the record below, or in your own notes. Add the goal to your My goals and projects document so it is ready for your next conversation.

Your private practice record

Record a short observation in your own words. You do not need to paste a full AI conversation. This record stays separate from context copied to your AI; the backup includes it so you can keep learning across visits.

Typing keeps a temporary draft in this browser tab only. Before you close the tab, copy your record into your own notes. When signed in, you can also choose Save to My context to keep it in Moodle, or use the private practice editor without JavaScript.

5. Finish: check you can return

Close your notes, then open your my-context folder again. Find your three documents and your next goal. Copy the useful part of one document into a new AI conversation. If you saved to My context, sign in again and find your documents there too.

You have finished when you can find your documents again, explain why you chose particular context, name one checked result and one correction, and describe your next goal. Exploring a fictional example is a valid start; label it and plan a real trial when you can.

Signed in? Saving text does not complete an activity. Share only a brief, non-sensitive reflection if asked.
